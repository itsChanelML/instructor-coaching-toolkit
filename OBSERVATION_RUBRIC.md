# Instructor Observation Rubric

Used to observe a live (or recorded) technical session and produce feedback
that's specific enough to act on. Eight dimensions, each scored on the same
four-level scale. There is no overall average — see "How to score," below,
for why.

## The scale

| Level | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Not yet** | This didn't happen, or happened in a way that actively worked against learning. |
| **Developing** | Present, but inconsistent or effortful — works, but the room can tell it's effortful. |
| **Solid** | Consistently good. This is the bar for certification. |
| **Exemplary** | Worth another instructor watching specifically to learn from it. |

## The eight dimensions

### 1. Technical accuracy
Is the content correct and current? When a question exposes something the
instructor doesn't know, do they say so and follow up — or guess and hope?
*Not yet looks like:* confidently wrong, and it isn't caught before the room
internalizes it.
*Exemplary looks like:* "I don't know — let me check and post the answer in
the channel by end of day" said without any loss of authority.

### 2. Pacing & time management
Does the session protect the load-bearing sections' time budget, even when
something upstream ran long? Does the instructor notice — mid-session, not
at the retro — that the room is behind or ahead, and adjust?
*Not yet looks like:* a fixed script run regardless of room signal; the last
20 minutes get compressed into 3.

### 3. Clarity of explanation
Do explanations build from what the room already knows, in the room's own
vocabulary, rather than the instructor's internal mental model of the
system? Is jargon defined at first use?
*Not yet looks like:* correct explanations that only make sense to someone
who already understood the concept before the explanation started.

### 4. Checking for understanding
Real checks — a question with a wrong-answer-shaped option, a "show me on
your screen," a poll — rather than "does that make sense?" (a question
almost nobody answers honestly).
*Exemplary looks like:* catching a room-wide misunderstanding from a check
*before* it surfaces as twenty confused Slack messages during the lab.

### 5. Hands-on lab facilitation
Walking the room (physically or via breakout rooms) rather than sitting at
the front waiting for hands to raise. Distinguishing "stuck on a typo" from
"stuck on the concept" quickly, and responding to each differently.
*Not yet looks like:* the same three people ask questions and everyone else
is silently and invisibly stuck.

### 6. Handling questions
Answering the question asked, not the question the instructor wishes had
been asked. Redirecting a rabbit-hole without making the asker feel shut
down ("great tangent — let's take it after class so we don't lose the room").
*Not yet looks like:* visible impatience, or answering in a way that punishes
someone for asking.

### 7. Engagement & energy
Presence that reads as genuine interest in the material, not a performance
of enthusiasm. Noticing and responding to a flagging room (a break, a change
of pace, a joke) rather than pushing through on the original plan.

### 8. Inclusivity & accessibility
Pacing and phrasing that doesn't silently leave people behind (assumed prior
tools, assumed OS, assumed English fluency). Never making a struggling
learner an example in front of the room, even a well-intentioned one.
*Not yet looks like:* "this part's easy" said before something that clearly
wasn't easy for at least a third of the room.

## How to score

**Score every dimension independently — do not average them into one
number.** A single "Not yet" on accuracy or accessibility is disqualifying
for certification regardless of how exemplary everything else is; those two
are the ones that actively harm learners rather than merely under-deliver.
The other six can offset each other somewhat in a coaching conversation, but
none of them should be waved through silently either — see
`FEEDBACK_TEMPLATE.md` for how to write up a dimension that needs work
without it reading as a verdict on the person.

**Certification bar:** *Solid* or better on all eight dimensions, observed
across at least two sessions (see `TRAIN_THE_TRAINER_PROGRAM.md`) — one
good day is not evidence of a repeatable skill.
